Featured · SS 2025 · Design Excellence Award

Velare

Carolina Theatre Addition · Uptown Charlotte, NC · Prof. David Thaddeus · Partner: Danielle Heckler

Back to Portfolio

Velare is a contemporary addition to Uptown Charlotte's historic Carolina Theatre (1927), designed to connect a newly introduced front-facing program with the theatrical experience of the existing building.

The project draws from the mechanics of a theater curtain — specifically the valence and traveler — to inform both façade and form. The building's wave-like massing echoes a curtain in motion, while glass surfaces drape at balcony recesses, acting as a valence from the floor above. A white exterior functions as a traveler, selectively opening to reveal interior moments.

Through this gesture, the building positions its occupants as performers and the surrounding city as the audience, blurring the boundary between theater and street. Velare activates its first seven floors with public-facing programs including a restaurant, coffee shops, an art gallery, and an auditorium, while the upper eight floors are dedicated to flexible office space.

Wave Station

Charlotte Gateway Station Proposal · Prof. David Thaddeus

Back to Portfolio

Wave Station is a proposal for Charlotte's long-anticipated Gateway Station, envisioned as a transitional threshold between two distinct urban conditions. On one side lies a mix of residential, educational, and retail spaces; on the other, a dense and active metropolitan core.

The project bridges these environments through both architectural form and outdoor programming. The building's flowing geometry guides movement across the site, while a public park creates space for rest and pause — offering locals a retreat from daily routines and providing visitors with a welcoming arrival experience.

Internally, the station supports this transition through spaces for rest, performance, and dining. The structure introduces three new rail lines while significantly extending the reach of Charlotte's public transportation network. Arrival is defined by a sweeping façade where exposed steel supports carry a wave-like roof that guides movement and sightlines inward toward the Charlotte skyline.

Catasta

SRO Housing Proposal · Prof. Mark Manac · Partners: L. Hernandez, K. Truong, O. Ata

Back to Portfolio

Charlotte's rapid gentrification has intensified the need for affordable housing that supports long-term stability and community growth. Catasta responds with a mixed-use development that combines supportive housing, social services, and commercial programming to create a resilient and inclusive urban environment.

Centered around Single Room Occupancy (SRO) units, the design prioritizes affordability while integrating spaces for counseling, job training, and healthcare. Ground-level commercial and community spaces activate the street and strengthen connections between residents and the surrounding neighborhood.

By carefully balancing private living spaces with shared public amenities, the project enhances livability while contributing to the ongoing revitalization of the Wilmore neighborhood.

Paramorphic

Chemistry Design Build · Prof. Rachel Dickey · Partners: K. Truong, A. Chaaraoui, C. Ly-Hang

Back to Portfolio

Paramorphic is a design-build project that explores parametric form as a tool for creating an interactive chemistry pop-up environment for children. The project focuses on translating complex scientific concepts into engaging, hands-on experiences through form, color, and materiality.

The design process involved material selection, construction experimentation, and the development of multiple interactive components. Four key intents guided the process — sit, demo, display, and shade — each individually studied, prototyped, and refined through physical models prior to integration into the final installation.

We initially explored kerfing wood to achieve curvature, but encountered limitations when scaling up. In response, we shifted to a parabolic curve and began experimenting with tensioned string systems. The project culminated in a 1:1 built installation and an informative animation documenting the full process from material study to final form.

Case Study · FW 2025

Water Cherry House

Kengo Kuma · Prof. Chris Jarret · Japanese Spaces & Concepts

Back to Portfolio

Water Cherry House by Kengo Kuma was studied as a case study in a Japanese Spaces and Concepts course. The project began with an intensive research exercise that challenged us to locate and understand the house using only publicly available images — without drawings or maps.

This process evolved into detailed diagramming and 3D modeling developed entirely from visual analysis. From this foundation, the project expanded into plans, sections, axonometric drawings, and a 3D printed model. The course emphasized precision, intent, and attention to detail at every stage.

While renderings were not required, I chose to produce them to further explore and communicate the project's material and spatial nuances. The Water Cherry House responds directly to its surrounding cherry trees, sloped terrain, and the river that runs through the site — broken into smaller volumes that engage the landscape through an approach rooted in traditional Japanese architecture where space, sequence, and nature are carefully intertwined.
